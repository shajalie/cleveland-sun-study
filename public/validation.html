What this study can tell you

This is a physically based reconstruction for comparing the house and garden under specified daylight conditions. It predicts light transport through an estimated building. It has not been calibrated against on-site brightness measurements, so it cannot establish exactly how bright the real house will feel.

One scene, two renderers

Blender Cycles generates the editable scene and 512-sample reference images on the RTX GPU. The browser traces the same exported geometry and material textures with multiple importance sampling, up to 20 bounces and 512 or 2048 samples. It traces diffuse reflections, glossy reflections, glass refraction, tree and building obstruction, and water reflections. The initial still is labeled as a Cycles reference; interacting enters the live renderer.

Clear lighting uses Blender's Nishita atmosphere, with air, dust and ozone densities set to 1, an estimated 90 m elevation and a 0.53° solar disk. Skies are rendered at 4096 × 2048, then area-averaged to shared 2048 × 1024 HDR maps. The disk is inside the HDR, and Appearance has no additional Sun light. There are four 2026 seasonal dates and three local times per date; there is no time interpolation.

Overcast uses the CIE standard overcast distribution: zenith radiance is three times horizon radiance. Its global horizontal illumination is set to 35% of the corresponding clear scenario. This is an explicit cloudy-weather assumption, not measured Washington weather. The ground's contribution is traced from geometry; the lower sky hemisphere adds no artificial fill.

Both engines apply the same global HDR scale of 1/30 and the same display curve, sRGB(E L / (1 + E L)), with E = 8 at the default +3 EV. The camera does not automatically brighten dark rooms. These exposure labels are relative display settings, not calibrated photographic EV100. Pixel values are never presented as lux.

Where the uncertainty matters

InputEvidence and assumption
Plan and orientationSupplied meter-scale indoor plan and survey-foot outdoor plan; stored orientation vectors retained. Overall layout is a reconstruction, not a survey. The registered footprint is retained; modeled living-room depth is about 8.4 m versus the listing label of 25 ft (7.62 m). This roughly 10% discrepancy remains unresolved and affects light penetration.
WindowsOpenings from the supplied model, refined using listing photos. Broad rear sunroom glazing and arched landing window and powder/upstairs bathroom windows are photo-inferred. Dimensions, glass products and actual visible transmission remain unknown.
GlassDielectric IOR 1.5, estimated normal transmission 82% or 55%. Cycles uses straight attenuated shadow paths through thin parallel glass to avoid noisy refractive caustics, while visible paths refract and reflect. Browser transmission is an approximate BSDF. The 12% covering option is attenuation only; it does not reproduce woven-curtain scattering.
TreesEstimated positions and heights, explicit leaf and branch geometry. Crowns can be scaled 70–125%. Species, leaf density and seasonal leaf loss are not measured or seasonally modeled. Winter defaults to a conservative leaf-on scenario; the Leaf-off bound removes all leaves but retains trunks and branches. Real winter conditions depend on species.
Walls and surfacesApproximate linear plaster reflectance 0.72, ceiling 0.82, timber floor 0.25/0.12/0.055 RGB; textured rough surfaces. Furnishings, joinery and material colors are photo-informed approximations.
ExteriorHouse envelope, shallow hipped roof, porches and neighbor masses obstruct the same light used indoors. Rear terraces have estimated 0.8 m and 0.4 m levels. Remaining terrain is simplified. Pool water has dielectric reflection/refraction; underwater caustics are not validated.
CoverageMain and second floors plus front, rear, pool surround and side areas. Basement and physical stair flight are omitted. Floor buttons bridge the stairs. Pool and planting exclude teleportation.

Using the result

Compare places at the same date, time, sky, glazing and exposure. Let live rays settle; Fine detail renders at full resolution and takes longer. Save comparisons after at least 128 samples. The sample count describes computation, not guaranteed convergence. Direct sun is a separate normalized directional-shadow diagnostic with no diffuse sky or reflected bounces, not a brightness or lux map.

The most valuable future measurements are window dimensions and transmission, obstruction/tree heights, and simultaneous indoor/outdoor illuminance under a recorded sky. Listing photographs help reconstruct geometry but their exposure, editing and electric lights make them unsuitable as absolute brightness measurements.
