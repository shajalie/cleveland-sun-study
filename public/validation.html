What this study can tell you

This is a physically based reconstruction for comparing the house and garden under specified daylight conditions. It predicts light transport through an estimated building. It has not been calibrated against on-site brightness measurements, so it cannot establish exactly how bright the real house will feel.

Precomputed tour and live rays

The default tour uses Cycles diffuse lighting baked onto a second set of surface coordinates. It stores direct sunlight and indirect diffuse bounces without freezing the camera. Four seasonal dates, three local times and clear/overcast skies provide 24 discrete scenarios. Walking does not recalculate those light paths. Reflections and transparent surfaces use a cheaper, view-dependent approximation; this mode is not full live path tracing.

The live ray modes remain available for glass and crown-size sensitivity checks. They trace the exported scene on the host GPU and take longer to settle. The phone receives JPEG frames and sends controls; it does not load the 3D scene or compute light paths. The host stops drawing an unchanged baked view.

Clear lighting uses Blender's Nishita atmosphere, with air, dust and ozone densities set to 1, an estimated 90 m elevation and a 0.53° solar disk. Skies are rendered at 4096 × 2048, then area-averaged to shared 2048 × 1024 HDR maps. The disk is inside the HDR, and Appearance has no additional Sun light. There are four 2026 seasonal dates and three local times per date; there is no time interpolation.

Overcast uses the CIE standard overcast distribution: zenith radiance is three times horizon radiance. Its global horizontal illumination is set to 35% of the corresponding clear scenario. This is an explicit cloudy-weather assumption, not measured Washington weather. The ground's contribution is traced from geometry; the lower sky hemisphere adds no artificial fill.

The shared HDR scale is 1/30. The rebuilt tour uses AgX display tone mapping and fixed relative exposure, default +3 EV. The camera does not automatically brighten a room when you enter. Blender's reference contrast look differs slightly from the browser's AgX implementation. These are display settings, not calibrated photographic EV100; pixels are not lux.

Phone streaming and surface detail

The host displays precomputed diffuse daylight while moving, then replaces a stationary view with a 128-sample Blender Cycles render. The phone receives JPEG images and sends controls; it does not render the 3D model. Native views are cached. Normal maps retain plaster grain, leather pores, fabric weave and stone wear; roof tiles and lawn blades have modeled geometry. Material classes and scales are estimated from photographs, not scanned from this property.

Architectural glass uses attenuated straight-through shadow rays and dielectric camera/reflection paths. This thin-pane approximation avoids undersampled sun caustics; assumed normal-incidence transmission is 82%. Higher fixed camera exposure can reveal material detail in shaded rooms. It changes the display, not the light energy.

Using the result

Compare places at the same date, time, sky, glazing and exposure. On the phone, wait until the status says Blender Cycles before judging stationary detail. Browser live-ray diagnostics are separate and can take a long time to initialize on this detailed model. The sample count describes computation, not guaranteed convergence. Direct sun is a separate normalized directional-shadow diagnostic with no diffuse sky or reflected bounces, not a brightness or lux map.

The most valuable future measurements are window dimensions and transmission, obstruction/tree heights, and simultaneous indoor/outdoor illuminance under a recorded sky. Listing photographs help reconstruct geometry but their exposure, editing and electric lights make them unsuitable as absolute brightness measurements.
